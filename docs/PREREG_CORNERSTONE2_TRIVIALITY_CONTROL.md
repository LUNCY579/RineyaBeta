# Preregistration — the triviality control for Cornerstone 2's score

**Version 2, frozen before any campaign data existed.** Written 2026-09-02 on
`research/cornerstone2-v1`, revised 2026-09-03 after a six-lens adversarial design
review found faults that would have invalidated the v1 design. **No arm of this
campaign had been run when v2 was written** — the only GPU contact was a 2-case CPU
smoke test of the plumbing. The full changelog is §10; nothing above it was edited
silently.

Everything below — arms, endpoint, stratification, statistics, decision rule, abort
conditions and the predicted values — is fixed at the commit that adds this file. Any
later change is an amendment and must be recorded in §10 with its date, its reason,
and whether data had been seen.

---

## 1. The question

Cornerstone 2 ranks cached positions by accumulated attention mass `S / max(C,1)^e`
and then max-pools that score **backward** before top-k. Every published contrast
compares the mechanism to *earlier versions of itself* (centred vs causal, fixed vs
derived reach) or to arms with no ranking at all (`random`, `streaming`).

O11 already established that the score is **not** an intrinsic-importance measure: it
is *similarity to the question*. Substituting a wrong question at scoring time drops
8K passkey from 1.0000 to 0.0556 and 0.0000 on two independent wrong queries.

That resolution creates the question this preregistration exists to answer:

> **Does accumulated-attention significance do anything that a trivial selector —
> computable from token ids alone, before any forward pass — could not?**

This is the question that killed the previous programme. DAST died because its
supervised quantity was recoverable from the layer-0 token embedding: trivially
available, so no clever placement of it could matter. If a trivial selector matches
significance here, the score is this programme's `L_state`, and the contribution is
far narrower than the paper claims.

**Run this before anything else, because a null here changes what every other
experiment is worth.**

Relation to `PLAN_LONG_REASONING.md` P4: P4 asks whether the score *loses its margin*
when query and evidence share no words. This asks the converse — whether a trivial
selector *gains* the score's margin when they do. P4 remains unrun and this does not
substitute for it (§9).

### 2.1 What "trivial" means, and it is enforced

A trivial arm may read **only** the record's `question` string, the prompt's token
ids, and structure the prompt itself prints. It may not touch
`support_document_ids`, the gold phrase, `decision`, or the needle depth. All of
D, E, G satisfy this; it is a property of the code, which never receives those
fields.

* **lexical-naive** — 1.0 at every position whose token id occurs in the tokenised
  question, 0.0 elsewhere.
* **lexical-idf** — `log(n/df(t))` normalised to [0,1], `df` = the token's count in
  *this* prompt. Parameter-free.
* **lexical-doc-bm25** — segment the prompt on the record delimiters it already
  prints (`[doc_… | trust=… | updated_at=…]`), stopping at `</FACTUAL_EVIDENCE>`;
  score each record against the question with textbook BM25 (k1=1.5, b=0.75) at
  **record-level** document frequency; broadcast a record's score to its positions.

All three pass through **exactly the same machinery as the score** — same sink mask,
same backward max-pool at (6,0), same recent window, same top-k, same slot count.
Only the ranking signal differs.

### 2.2 Why arm G exists, and why the v1 design without it was unsound

v1's decisive arm was per-position IDF. Measured on the exact records this campaign
runs (CPU, tokenizer only, gold-span retention after a keep-0.25 eviction, answer
cases only):

| trivial selector | 4K | 16K |
|---|---:|---:|
| lexical-naive | 0.354 | — |
| lexical-idf (v1's decisive arm) | **0.736** | **0.681** |
| **lexical-doc-bm25 (arm G)** | **1.000** | **1.000** |

Arm G also ranks the *true support document* first in 142/144 and 65/72 cases. Two
consequences:

1. Per-position IDF is not the strongest trivial baseline, and it is not even really
   IDF — `df` is a term count inside one document, i.e. inverse collection frequency
   on a collection of size one. **A SUBSTANTIVE verdict resting on arm D alone would
   have been unsound**, because an obvious variant of the same idea does far better.
2. The gain comes entirely from using the *document* as the unit — which is precisely
   what this paper's own §1 argues is the right unit of selection.

A segmentation bug found by this repository's own unit test is recorded in §10: the
last record initially ran to the end of the prompt and swallowed the `<QUESTION>`
block, so the trailing distractor contained the query verbatim and won BM25 on every
case. The gold span survived anyway, so span retention did **not** expose it; the test
asserts on the answer record's *score*, which did.

### 2.3 Why arm N is the floor, and arm F is not

`random` is substituted **after** the pool, so the pool is inert for it and it keeps
scattered singletons. Every lexical arm is substituted **before** the pool, so even
its tie-break noise is max-pooled into contiguous runs — and contiguity alone
preserves spans. Measured with a **constant** weight vector carrying zero lexical
information:

| floor | 4K | 16K |
|---|---:|---:|
| `random`, post-pool (arm F) | 0.014 | 0.014 |
| `random-prepool`, constant weight (arm N) | **0.097** | **0.042** |

That difference is *plumbing*, not signal, and `R` computed against F would charge it
to the lexical arms. **R is therefore computed against N.** F is retained only for
comparability with the committed random arms.

The effect is real but small — it does not come close to explaining arm D's 0.736.
The review that raised it estimated the constant-weight arm at 0.550/0.600; direct
measurement puts it at 0.097/0.042, about 6× smaller. The smaller number is the one
used here.

### 2.4 Arms reused rather than re-run

`long4k-random-k0.25__rag_long4k` and `long16k-random-k0.25__rag_long16k` are already
committed at *exactly* this campaign's configuration (keep 0.25, recent-frac 0.15,
sinks 4, pool 7, back 6, fwd 0, seed 20260826, base core). They serve as arm F,
subject to the same manifest diff (§6.2). They may be used **only if** the
reproduction gate (§6.1) passes; otherwise arm F is reported as not run.

Arm A is re-run at full n despite also existing, because it is the reference every
contrast uses and re-running it turns §6.1 into a 200-case byte-identity check rather
than a 10-case one.

### 2.5 Multiplicity

The decision statistic uses **the best of D, E and G**. Taking a maximum over three
arms inflates the chance of the *trivial-wins* verdict, never the *score-is-real*
verdict. The multiplicity is deliberately pointed against this programme's preferred
conclusion, so it is not corrected.

## 3. Endpoint, stratification and statistics

**Endpoint: `contract_fulfillment_score >= 0.999`** — the required phrase is literally
present in the reply, a per-case binary. The composite `quality_score` is **not** the
endpoint: `configs/quality_evaluation.json` sets
`reference_similarity_is_diagnostic_only = true` and the composite is ~54%
mechanical/fluency. It is reported alongside, labelled diagnostic. (The 2-case smoke
test already shows the divergence: two replies with `contract_fulfillment_score = 1.0`
scored `quality_score = 0.0`.)

### 3.1 Stratification — the primary statistic is the ANSWER stratum

**This is a change from v1 and it matters.** The slices are not one task:

| | answer | abstain |
|---|---:|---:|
| rag_long4k (200) | **144** | 56 |
| rag_long16k (100) | **72** | 28 |

The 28% `abstain` cases require reproducing a canned refusal — `"I don't know based on
the available information."` — whose text lives **only in the 66-token system prompt**,
at depth ≈ 0.01, inside the protected sink region. Nothing about them tests evidence
retention, no lexical arm can retain an evidence span that does not exist, and
including them compresses every arm toward a common constant.

**The primary statistic is computed on `decision == "answer"` only: n = 144 (4K) and
n = 72 (16K).** The abstain stratum is reported separately as a secondary — a
compression policy that destroys abstention is a real defect, and it would be hidden
by pooling, not revealed by it.

**Strict cases only** within each stratum — at least one contract requirement, not
`open_ended`. On these slices that is 200/200 and 100/100, so the filter is a no-op;
it is applied anyway so the definition is fixed rather than convenient.

### 3.2 Tests

**Paired exact McNemar** (two-sided, discordant pairs only), paired on case id.
**Exact 95% CI on the paired difference**, conditioning on the discordant count and
inverting the Clopper-Pearson interval; the rule of three when discordance is zero.
Both are implemented without scipy and validated against `R`'s `binom.test` to five
decimals and against this programme's own published p-values (117/83 → 0.0194).

**Prespecified comparisons, in order.** Primary: **A vs L**, where L is the best of
D/E/G. Secondary: A vs G, A vs D, A vs E, A vs B, A vs C, L vs N, D vs N, F vs N. No
other contrast may be promoted to a headline after seeing the data.

## 4. Preregistered predictions

From CPU simulation of the selection geometry (tokenizer only, no model weights),
gold-span retention on answer cases:

| | naive | IDF | doc-BM25 | random-prepool | random |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| rag_long4k | 0.354 | 0.736 | 1.000 | 0.097 | 0.014 |
| rag_long16k | — | 0.681 | 1.000 | 0.042 | 0.014 |

Span retention is not the endpoint — the model can answer without the whole span and
fail with it — so these are **predictions to be scored, not results**:

* **P-A.** G > D > E on the hard endpoint at both lengths.
* **P-B.** G lands at or above A. Committed point prediction: G ≥ 0.75 at 4K against
  A ≈ 0.82 on the answer stratum.
* **P-C.** B (centred) < A at both lengths — the directionality result transfers from
  passkey to language. **Never measured on language; it could fail.**
* **P-D.** C (no-pool) < A at both lengths, and C > N.
* **P-E.** R (§7) ≥ 0.90 at 4K, i.e. the TRIVIAL or PARTIAL branch. **This is a
  reversal of v1's prediction** (v1 predicted R ∈ [0.4, 0.7] with only arm D in play);
  the reversal is caused by adding arm G and is recorded before any data.

Recording these now means the analysis cannot be re-narrated afterwards as "what we
expected all along". **If P-E holds, the central claim of this programme is in
serious trouble, and that is the point of running it first.**

## 5. Power

Exact-McNemar power against the discordant count:

| discordant pairs | 6 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 50 | 80 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| power at p(favour A) = 0.8 | 0.262 | 0.376 | 0.804 | 0.939 | 0.994 | 1.000 |
| power at p(favour A) = 0.9 | 0.531 | 0.736 | 0.989 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

A fully one-sided result reaches p < 0.05 at **6/0**. Power is non-monotonic at very
small discordance (6 → 8 pairs falls from 0.262 to 0.168) purely from the discreteness
of the exact test; noted so a marginal cell is not misread.

Exact 95% CI half-widths on the paired difference, **on the answer stratum**:

| | 0 discordant | 4 | 10 | 20 | 40 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| n = 144 (4K) | 2.08 pp | 2.40 pp | 4.35 pp | 6.33 pp | 9.00 pp |
| n = 72 (16K) | 4.17 pp | 4.80 pp | 8.69 pp | 12.67 pp | 18.00 pp |

**The equivalence verdict may only be declared from the 4K cell.** At n = 144 the
±10 pp bound survives up to 48 balanced discordant pairs (33% of cases), so the
TRIVIAL branch is reachable — checked by enumeration, not assumed. At n = 72 it
survives only 12 pairs (17%), so 16K is a **direction** cell, not a certification
cell.

The endpoint has ample dynamic range: uncompressed 0.860 at 4K and 0.690 at 16K,
significance 0.820 / 0.700, random 0.135 / 0.180, streaming 0.000 — all reproduced
from committed per-case records before this document was written. Neither floor- nor
ceiling-bound. No MDE-limited surrogate is involved.

## 6. Validity gates — binding

### 6.1 Reproduction gate

The environment carries `transformers 5.15.0` / `torch 2.13.0+cu130`, not necessarily
the state the committed arms were produced under. Arm A is run first at full n and its
replies compared byte-for-byte with the committed
`long4k-sig-k0.25-rf0.15__rag_long4k` / `long16k-sig-k0.25-rf0.15__rag_long16k`.

* **all identical** → proceed; the campaign inherits the committed arms' provenance
  and arm F may be reused (§2.4).
* **any differ** → the campaign still runs, because all arms share one code state and
  remain internally comparable, but **no result may be compared to a committed number
  from the earlier code state**, arm F is reported not run, and the discrepancy is
  reported as a finding in its own right.

(The `transformers 5` chat-template hazard was checked directly and does **not** apply
to this call form: `apply_chat_template(..., tokenize=False)` returns `str` here.)

### 6.2 Manifest diff gate

Every arm's `run_manifest.json` is diffed against arm A's. Valid only if each arm
differs in **exactly** the fields §2 says it should (`tag`, `select`; plus the pooling
fields for B and C), together with the free-running fields `elapsed_s`, `recent`,
`recent_effective_mean`, `heavy_zero_count`, `case_count`.

**`mean_kept_ratio` is NOT free-running.** It was whitelisted in v1, which would have
let an arm that never evicted (ratio ≈ 1.0037) pass the gate and be scored as a
policy. It is now asserted at 0.25 ± 0.01 for every arm.

### 6.3 Degenerate-policy gate

`heavy_zero_count` must be 0 for every arm.

### 6.4 Selection-actually-differs gate

Each non-reference arm must differ from A's replies on at least one case. An arm whose
output is identical to A's throughout is a plumbing failure, not a null. Asserted in
`scripts/kv/test_lexical_select.py` on the real eviction path before the campaign.

### 6.5 Dynamic-range gate

`R` is defined only when `A − N ≥ 0.40` **and** `A > N`. If either fails, the campaign
is an instrument failure: **no verdict of any kind**, and the discrepancy is the only
reported result. Without this, an environment collapse that drops every arm to the
floor yields `R = 1.0`, `p = 1.0` and a rule-of-three CI of 2.08 pp — printing
**TRIVIAL**, the maximally destructive verdict, from a run in which nothing was
measured. Committed values give `A − F = 0.685` (4K) and `0.520` (16K), so the gate is
far from binding in the normal case; it exists for the abnormal one.

### 6.6 Arm-G structure gate

Arm G raises if a prompt yields fewer than two record delimiters before
`</FACTUAL_EVIDENCE>`. It is undefined on `rag_dev` (one record) and that is enforced,
not worked around.

## 7. Decision rule — FIXED BEFORE THE DATA

On the **answer stratum** at a given length, with

```
A = significance      N = contiguity-matched random floor
L = max(D, E, G)      the best trivial selector
R = (L - N) / (A - N)     the trivial-recovery fraction
```

R is the share of significance's advantage over an unranked, contiguity-matched
control that a selector needing no forward pass already captures. Reported with a
bootstrap 95% CI (10,000 paired case resamples, seed 20260902) and with the fraction
of resamples whose denominator vanished; **R is not reportable if that fraction
exceeds 5%** or if §6.5 fails.

**Primary verdict, from the 4K cell — the only one powered for equivalence:**

| condition | verdict |
|---|---|
| L > A with McNemar p < 0.05 | **BEATEN.** A selector with no forward pass outperforms the mechanism. Reported as the headline. |
| R ≥ 0.90 **and** p ≥ 0.05 **and** the 95% CI on (A − L) lies within ±10 pp | **TRIVIAL.** The score is this programme's `L_state`. Claims about "accumulated importance" must be withdrawn. |
| R ≥ 0.90 **and** A > L at p < 0.05 | **PARTIAL (upper).** The residual is statistically real but bounded at ≤10% of the advantage. Re-scope the contribution to the residual and quantify it. |
| 0.50 ≤ R < 0.90 | **PARTIAL.** A trivial selector recovers most of the advantage. Re-scope. |
| R < 0.50 **and** A > L at p < 0.05 | **SUBSTANTIVE.** The score does work no trivial selector reproduces *on this benchmark*, bounded by §9. |
| anything else | **INCONCLUSIVE.** Report the numbers; close nothing. |

The third row and the catch-all close a hole in v1: `R ≥ 0.90` with `p < 0.05` matched
no row at all, and that band — "a trivial index recovers 90–99.9% of the advantage but
the residual is real" — is exactly the alternative §1 says the experiment exists to
detect. v1's own worked minimum-detectable example (6/0 discordant → R = 0.956,
p = 0.031) landed in the hole.

**R is reported twice**, and both go in any write-up:

* **R_structural** = over max(D, E, G) — the primary, above.
* **R_positional** = over max(D, E) — what a trivial selector achieves when the
  prompt has no parseable record structure to exploit. This separates "the score is
  replaceable" from "the score is replaceable *on prompts that print their own
  document boundaries*".

**Secondary verdicts, independent of the above:**

* **B (centred) vs A.** If A > B at p < 0.05 at both lengths, the directionality claim
  transfers to language and §4.10 of the paper gains its missing ablation. **If B ≥ A
  at either length, the paper's central claim does not hold at length, and that
  belongs in the abstract, not a limitations section.** Because the two lengths are
  the same items (§9.2), "both lengths" is a consistency check, not two independent
  tests.
* **C (no-pool) vs A.** What span inheritance is worth at length, independent of
  direction.
* **Abstain stratum.** Reported per arm. Any arm losing >10 pp of abstention against A
  is flagged; abstention collapse is a real defect.

**No verdict may be emitted unless §8's coverage minimum is met.**

## 8. Minimum coverage, and abort conditions

Coverage is enforced **per cell**, resolving a v1 contradiction (v1 required all arms
at *both* lengths and simultaneously promised that a 4K-only campaign yields a 4K
verdict). Within a cell, the analyser refuses a verdict unless:

1. Arms A, B, C, D, E, G, N are all present for that cell, with that cell's exact
   `case_count` (200 / 100). Arm F is optional; if absent, F-relative rows are
   reported "not run".
2. The paired intersection with arm A equals the full n for every arm.
3. Gates §6.2–§6.6 pass for that cell.
4. No arm's `results.jsonl` is shorter than its manifest's `case_count`.

A cell missing any required arm is reported **NOT RUN** and emits no verdict, primary
or secondary. **L = max(D, E, G) is undefined unless all three are present** — a
missing arm cannot be allowed to lower the maximum, since P-A predicts G is the max
and its absence would bias R downward, toward this programme's preferred conclusion.

If a run dies part-way its partial artifacts are **deleted, not analysed**. Multi-hour
GPU work on this host is unreliable, so arms run one at a time, each ~7–18 min, each a
complete unit of evidence.

## 9. What this experiment cannot conclude

Stated now, so it cannot be quietly dropped later.

1. **This benchmark is lexically favourable by construction.** Every `rag_long*`
   record carries `metadata.grounding.lexical_support_fraction = 1.0`, the question
   names the entity the answer document names, and the prompt prints its own record
   delimiters. A high R here is partly a fact about the dataset. A **low** R is the
   more informative outcome, obtained on the terrain most favourable to the trivial
   arms. `R_positional` is reported precisely so the structural advantage can be
   subtracted.
2. **The two lengths are the same items.** The 100 `rag_long16k` records are the same
   source questions, in the same order, as the first 100 `rag_long4k` records
   (verified: `src16 == src4[:100]`). 16K is a *length* replication on one item set,
   not an independent sample. Any "at both lengths" statement is a consistency check.
   The slice is also a contiguous block of 15 topics (8 at 16K) taken in file order,
   not a random sample of the 732 available.
3. It does not test P4. Whether the *score* survives at zero lexical overlap needs a
   dataset where question and evidence share no content words. Unrun.
4. One model, one depth (0.5), one keep fraction (0.25), one seed. The capable-model
   arms (`baya-ad`) are excluded: their adapter lives outside this repository.
5. It measures capability, not cost. No cost measurement from this repository may be
   cited as evidence the mechanism works.

## 10. Amendments

### v2 — 2026-09-03, before any campaign data existed

Prompted by a six-lens adversarial design review (six finders, adversarial
verification, run before GPU time per the standing rule). Every change below was made
with **zero** campaign observations in hand. Claims the review made that I could not
independently reproduce are recorded as rejected, with the measured value.

**Accepted and acted on:**

1. **Arm G added.** Arm D was not the strongest trivial baseline; a document-level
   BM25 selector using the prompt's own delimiters retains the gold span 1.000/1.000
   against D's 0.736/0.681, and ranks the true support document first 142/144 and
   65/72. Independently reproduced with the shipped code. Without G, a SUBSTANTIVE
   verdict would have been unsound. (§2.2)
2. **Arm N added and made the floor.** Pre-pool substitution buys contiguity that
   post-pool `random` does not have, so F was an unmatched zero point. (§2.3)
3. **Primary statistic stratified to `decision == "answer"`.** 28% of each slice is a
   canned-abstention task keyed to the system prompt. Independently reproduced
   (144/56 and 72/28). (§3.1)
4. **Decision-rule hole closed** — `R ≥ 0.90` with `p < 0.05` matched no row, and that
   band is the alternative the experiment exists to detect. Added the PARTIAL (upper)
   row and an INCONCLUSIVE catch-all. (§7)
5. **Dynamic-range gate added** (§6.5): an all-arms-at-the-floor instrument failure
   previously mapped to TRIVIAL, and `A < N` previously produced a positive R from two
   negatives.
6. **`mean_kept_ratio` removed from the free-running whitelist** (§6.2): an arm that
   never evicted would otherwise have passed the manifest gate.
7. **Coverage made per cell** (§8), resolving a flat contradiction in v1's §8.
8. **§5's CI table recomputed** with the exact conditional method the analyser
   actually implements; v1 quoted a Wald approximation that was 3–5% too narrow.
9. **§9.2 added** — the 16K cell is the same 100 items as the first 100 of the 4K
   cell. v1 treated the two lengths as independent replications.
10. **P-E reversed** from `R ∈ [0.4, 0.7]` to `R ≥ 0.90`, as a direct consequence of
    adding arm G. Recorded before data.

**Found by this repository's own unit test while implementing the above, not by the
review:** arm G's segmentation initially ran the last record to the end of the prompt,
swallowing the `<QUESTION>` block, so the trailing distractor contained the query
verbatim and won BM25 on every case. Gold-span retention was 1.000 *with the bug*, so
the span-retention proxy could not see it; the test asserts on the answer record's
score instead. Fixed by bounding segmentation at `</FACTUAL_EVIDENCE>`.

**Rejected after independent measurement** — recorded because a wrong number that
survives into a design is worse than one that was never raised:

* *"A constant weight vector scores 0.550/0.600 span retention against F's
  0.025/0.000."* Measured: **0.097 / 0.042** against **0.014 / 0.014**. The direction
  is right and arm N was added because of it; the magnitude was ~6× overstated, and
  the claim that R therefore "measures a plumbing asymmetry" does not follow — the
  lexical signal (0.736) dominates the plumbing (0.097).
* *"~70% of arm D's heavy budget (median 72%, max 147%) is decided by the tie-break
  rather than by IDF."* Measured: **mean 16.2%, max 38.7%** at 4K and mean 16.8% at
  16K. A share of 147% is not arithmetically possible, which was the tell.

**Not accepted:** the review's suggestion to drop arm E. E is the literal form of the
control this campaign was asked for, it costs ~15 GPU-min, and dropping the weakest
member of a maximum cannot change the maximum but can change what a reader is able to
check.
